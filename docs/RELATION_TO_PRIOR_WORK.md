# Relation to prior work

Written Oct 22 for the one-pager and the video script. Sources: LLM-IDEA and Kosuge et al. read for this note (main text and limitations; LLM-IDEA's benchmark sections skimmed), the Karan and Persson, ARROWS3, Miura and Murphy papers from the earlier reading notes (PROGRESS, Oct 21 evening, and `docs/SYNTHESIS.md`, "What not to claim"), and the searches of Oct 21-22 (OpenAlex, Valency). The searches are limited (Valency does not index Kosuge or Karan and Persson), so every "not found" below means "not found in what we searched".

## Paragraph for the one-pager

Fitting kinetic models to time-resolved XRD and selecting among them with Bayesian or likelihood criteria is established: Kosuge et al. (2026) select among nine textbook solid-state rate laws for each step of a fast SrCrO3 oxidation using Bayesian free energy, and they state that the method gives no guidance on how to define the candidate set, so the researcher must propose it and the conclusions are limited to it. Agents that pair a language-model proposer with an identifiability check also exist (LLM-IDEA, 2026), but they were tested on simulated and textbook systems, not on measured solid-state data. What we add is the combination, run on published time-resolved XRD of a precursor synthesis (Chapman et al., TiO2 + Li2CO3): a language model proposes the parts of the reaction network, code fits every combination jointly across temperatures, a held-out temperature checks the choice, identifiability is reported next to the fit, and the model says what is unresolved and which experiment would settle it. We have one chemistry and two runs per model, and our automatic selection is inconsistent (members ranked 4th and 36th against the baseline's 11th), so we claim a working loop and useful candidate generation, not a validated mechanism.

## Closest work, and what differs

| Work | What it does | What it does not do, or what differs |
|---|---|---|
| Kosuge et al., J. Phys. Chem. C 130, 10245 (2026) | Bayesian model selection on isothermal synchrotron XRD (12 ms frames, 400 C, one run) of SrCrO2.75 to SrCrO3. Rietveld fractions become a reaction progress per step; nine standard rate laws (contracting, diffusion, nucleation forms, integrated g(alpha) = kt) are compared by marginal likelihood from replica-exchange Monte Carlo, with Gaussian noise. Finds the two steps differ in nucleation and growth | A solid-gas reaction of one compound at one temperature; the candidate set is fixed by hand and the paper says so ("the researcher must therefore propose reasonable candidate models a priori ... the analysis cannot be fully automated, and the conclusions are limited to the predefined model set"); no held-out temperature, no identifiability analysis, no next experiment. We use AICc and profile likelihood, not Bayesian free energy, so do not call our selection Bayesian |
| LLM-IDEA, Shetty and Braga-Neto, arXiv 2610.11253 (Oct 2026, preprint) | An LLM proposer (a five-role "Council") plus a deterministic identifiability engine: sensitivity-matrix rank, the named confounded parameter direction, and a three-way verdict (capability limit, resolvable inside the design class, certified exhausted for every experiment of that kind). Tested on ODEBench, DiscoverPhysics, textbook ODEs and a synthetic two-body testbed they built | No measured chemistry or materials data; local identifiability at an operating point; its own limits include eight seeds, shared memory between runs and dependence on the declared design class. We report Fisher and profile-likelihood identifiability beside model selection on real XRD data but do not certify class-level exhaustion. Our LTOPO synthetic example makes the same kind of point (fixed-hold endpoints cannot separate activation energy from the Avrami exponent, so change the kind of experiment) without their certificate |
| ARROWS3, Szymanski et al., Nat. Commun. 14, 6956 (2023) | Chooses precursor sets from thermodynamic driving force and learned pairwise reactions on endpoint XRD (4 h holds); source of our LTOPO and YBCO tables | Endpoints, not kinetics; no uncertainty or identifiability; no held-out test of a kinetic model. Our LTOPO use is descriptive |
| Hu et al. (Chapman), JACS 145, 26545 (2023), and Kamm et al., JACS 144, 11975 (2022) | The time-resolved data we fit (TiO2 + Li2CO3 at 700, 725 and 750 C), with Avrami analysis of the Li2TiO3 step | Their analysis is Avrami slopes and kinetic regimes; no model selection or network inference |
| Karan and Persson, Chem. Mater. (2026) | A forward simulator: first-principles thermodynamics plus a machine-learned transport model feeding a cellular-automaton powder-reaction model, compared qualitatively with published in situ XRD (Ba-Fe-O, Y-Fe-O) | Simulation, not inference from data; read only in the Oct 21 notes |
| Murphy et al., Model Discovery Agent (2026); LLM-ACES and CARTOGRAPH (as described in LLM-IDEA) | LLM-proposed models with information-gain experiment design, scored on held-out predictions in simulation (Murphy); disagreement-driven design and Jacobian-kernel identifiability (the other two) | Simulated systems. Information-gain planning and hidden-species inference are therefore not ours to claim (`docs/SYNTHESIS.md`) |
| Sci. Rep. 2023, "Bayesian framework for analyzing adsorption processes observed via time-resolved X-ray diffraction" (doi 10.1038/s41598-023-40573-z; abstract only) | Bayesian analysis of adsorption in MOFs, cited by Kosuge as the precedent | Adsorption, not synthesis |

## What we can and cannot say

Can say, with the search caveat above: we did not find prior work that combines, on real time-resolved XRD of a precursor synthesis, model-proposed network parts, a joint fit across temperatures, a held-out temperature test, an identifiability report and a next-experiment suggestion. Each ingredient exists elsewhere.

Cannot say: first Bayesian or likelihood-based model selection on time-resolved XRD (Kosuge); first pairing of an identifiability check with an LLM discovery agent (LLM-IDEA); first information-gain experiment design (Murphy); that our selection reliably beats a baseline (it ranked 4th and 36th against the baseline's 11th; `docs/NEXT_STEPS.md`); that LLM-IDEA's identifiability certificate or Kosuge's marginal likelihood is something we have.

## Why it matters for the demo

Kosuge's stated limitation is the gap the demo addresses: the Bayesian step is only as good as the candidate set a person writes down. The loop's value is proposing that set, and the held-out temperature is the check on it. LLM-IDEA gives the vocabulary for the LTOPO message: when one kind of experiment cannot separate explanations, the answer is a different kind of experiment (a time series), not more fitting.
