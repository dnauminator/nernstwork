# Datasets added Oct 20 2026

For Daniel. This covers the data-mining pass on branch `jarrod/investigator-pipeline`: what was added, what it changes in existing work, what still needs a person, and how it was checked. Nothing here is committed yet. Per-file detail lives in each folder's `meta.json` and `verification.json`. The short version of each file is in `data/real/README.md`.

## 1. What was added

16 new trajectory datasets in `data/real/` (3 tin-thesis, 13 Gao 2022), plus 37 inputs-only folders under `data/digitized_inputs/` (nine of them thesis tables transcribed from text, two derived from the Katsounaros Diag. 12.1 and 12.3 pages), plus one file of 67 sourced thesis claims in `data/evidence/`. All trajectories are in mM-N with time in minutes. No potential has been converted to RHE.

Sources:

- **Tin theses.** All the journal papers in the tin series are closed access. Three Aristotle University of Thessaloniki PhD theses on didaktorika.gr hold the same data and more: Polatides 2007 (doi 10.12681/eadd/15209), Katsounaros 2009 (doi 10.12681/eadd/19491) and Dortsiou 2009 (doi 10.12681/eadd/27551). Downloading a PDF needs a free account. Without one, the team viewed pages in the archive's online reader, captured 10 page images at 300 dpi and digitized those. It then stopped on purpose: the reader's host (freader.ekt.gr) has a robots.txt that disallows all crawling (`User-agent: *`, `Disallow: /`), and the archive's terms allow linking to content but not reproducing it (section 7). The page images stayed in session scratch and are not retained. The nine thesis tables and the claims file were transcribed from the reader's per-page text layers, which also stayed in session scratch. The remaining figures wait on the PDFs (section 3).
- **Gao et al. 2022**, Environ. Sci. Technol. 56, 11602 (doi 10.1021/acs.est.1c08442): CuO-coated Cu foam electrified-membrane cathode with an NH3 trap, main text and SI.
- **Willoughby et al. 2026**, ACS Electrochem. 2, 353 (doi 10.1021/acselectrochem.5c00405): SI only.
- **Barrera et al. 2023**, ACS Catal. 13, 4178 (doi 10.1021/acscatal.2c05136): the authors' Zenodo workbooks (doi 10.5281/zenodo.7713184) plus the SI.

Abbreviations: Ag/AgCl potentials are as reported, SCE potentials are without iR compensation, "raster" means agent-script digitization of an image (not WebPlotDigitizer), and "a_w_f" means accept_with_flags.

## 2. Findings that affect existing work

### W5's R3 removes nitrite twice as fast as the published model

Nothing in `worlds/`, `scripts/build_worlds.py`, `tests/` or `docs/SPEC.md` was changed. Evidence is in `data/digitized_inputs/tin_xcheck_verification.json` (checks B1-C4) and `polatides2007thesis_diag5p8_xcheck/meta.json`.

- **What the authors computed.** Thesis eqs 41-44 (printed p. 101) read d[NO2]/dt = k1[NO3] - k2[NO2] - 2 k3[NO2]^2 and d[N2]/dt = k3[NO2]^2, with N2 per molecule. The thesis says the curves were solved with a MATLAB function, and appendix 2 (printed pp. 195-196) lists it: `dx = [-k1*x(1); k1*x(1)-k2*x(2)-k3*x(2)^2; k2*x(2); 0.5*k3*x(2)^2]` in mM and min, with k3 searched over 4.50e-4 to 7.53e-4 mM-1 min-1. The printed k3, 7.66e-3 L mol-1 s-1 = 4.6e-4 mM-1 min-1, sits in that range. It is the nitrite-loss coefficient, not the eq. 44 N2-formation coefficient. Caveat: the equations come from the flattened text layer of the reader. The code listing is plain text and unambiguous.
- **The drawn curves agree.** An independent fit to every curve pixel in Diag. 5.8 (3632 pixel runs, no curve assignment) gives a nitrite-loss coefficient of 7.53e-3 L mol-1 s-1, 1.7 % below the printed value. The same fit gives k1 = 4.18e-4 s-1 and k2 = 1.77e-4 s-1, with median residual 0.03 mM.
- **What W5 does.** R3 is `2 NO2 -> 2 SINK`, mass action, k = 4.6e-4. The simulator subtracts coeff x rate, so nitrite goes at 9.2e-4 [NO2]^2, twice the authors' 4.6e-4 [NO2]^2. W5 reproduces the printed equations with the printed k3 (max difference 0.008 mM-N), but that pairing is not the model the authors ran.
- **Effect.** At the anchor potential (-1.39 V vs RHE, 50 mM, 0-120 min), W5 gives N2 = 23.1 mM-N at 120 min. Daniel's point is 16.8, the thesis point 16.4 and the drawn curve about 15.4. RMS against the drawn curves is 2.19 / 3.14 / 1.42 / 5.98 mM-N (NO3 / NO2 / NH3 / N2). With R3 = 2.3e-4 and R1 = 0.0251 min-1 it is 0.07 / 0.08 / 0.09 / 0.12. Halving R3 alone fixes most of the NO2, NH3 and N2 misfit. R1 = 0.0251 (the drawn curves' k1) instead of the printed 0.0294 fixes the NO3 misfit. The R1 choice is a judgment call (printed vs drawn); the R3 value is a convention error.
- **Why the test did not catch it.** `tests/test_simulate.py::test_w5_reproduces_polatides_fig3_at_reference_potential` multiplies the N2 series by 2. `data/real/polatides2005_fig3_sn85cu15_50mM.json` already stores N2 in mM-N (16.8 at 120 min), so the test compares against 33.6. With the 7.0 mM tolerance it passes either way: N2 RMS is 5.49 as written and 5.10 with correct units. `docs/SPEC.md` 7d repeats the doubled number ("23 mM-N at 120 min against 34 digitized, so the printed k3 undershoots"). In correct units W5 overshoots, 23.1 against 16.8.
- **Already in the repo.** SPEC 7c reports a fit of the digitized points with k3 = 4.3e-3 L mol-1 s-1. In the repo's convention that is R3 = 2.58e-4 mM-1 min-1, 0.56x W5's value.
- **Smaller items.** The world file gives R3's k unit as `min^-1`; it is second order, mM^-1 min^-1. W5's `design_space.potential_V_vs_RHE` is [-1.79, -1.39]. With W5's own conversion (-2.0 V vs Ag/AgCl + 0.197 + 0.059 x 7 = -1.39 V vs RHE) that is -2.4 to -2.0 V vs Ag/AgCl. Fig. 6 measured -1.6, -1.8, -1.9 and -2.0 V vs Ag/AgCl. So only the anchor, -2.0 V, lies in the measured window. Every other potential in the design space is more cathodic than any measured point, by up to 0.4 V at -2.4 V, and its alphas are extrapolated. The measured -1.6 to -1.9 V side is not in the design space at all. At -1.79 V vs RHE the effective R2 and R3 are 7.7x and 4.0x their anchor values.
- **Suggested changes, for you to decide.** Set R3 k to about 2.3e-4 mM^-1 min^-1 (2.26e-4 from the curves, 2.58e-4 from a fit to your points). Optionally set R1 to 0.0251. Drop the x2 in the test, and correct SPEC 7d and the unit label.

### Hidden pool in the W3/W4 run

The Katsounaros thesis measured two intermediates that the five-species 2012 dataset leaves out: hydroxylamine (colorimetric) and hyponitrite (ion chromatography). Diag. 12.1 plots their production and consumption rates, with those of NO2-, NH3, N2 and N2O, for the 0.5 M NaNO3 + 1 M NaOH run on Sn at -2.2 V. That this is the 2012 Fig. 1 run is inferred from the conditions, the times and the integrated rates reproducing the plotted concentrations; the thesis does not say so. The rates were digitized (`katsounaros2009thesis_diag12p1_rates`) and turned into concentrations (`katsounaros2009thesis_diag12p1_intermediates`, with README and `verification.json`). The deficit is 500 mM-N minus NO3, NO2, N2, N2O and NH3 in Daniel's file.

Model B X is the repo simulator with the W3 constants (paper Table 1). An independent verifier reproduced every number in the table with its own code.

- **The rates are finite differences, not instantaneous rates.** Each plotted rate is the mean of the two adjacent interval slopes of the sampled concentrations (a central difference on this grid, one-sided at the ends, as Origin's Differentiate does). Differentiating the thesis NO2- markers this way gives the plotted rates to 0.01 mM/min. Inverting the scheme reproduces the thesis's NO2-, N2 and N2O markers to 0.17-0.19 mM-N RMS; the trapezoid rule misses by 11.4, 1.9 and 0.7. NH2OH and hyponitrite are never plotted as concentrations, so their inversion cannot be checked directly.
- **Coverage.** The measured intermediates cover 10 % of the deficit at 30 min, rising to about 52 % at 180 min. Hyponitrite holds nearly all of that from 120 min on; at 90 min NH2OH is still 16 % of it (5.7 of 35.9 mM-N).
- **Unmeasured remainder.** It peaks at about 103 mM-N (20.6 % of N) at 30-60 min (98.8-111.2 across integration schemes and deficit sources) and falls to 38 mM-N by 180 min. Weighted by deficit it is two thirds of the loss, close to the thesis's "about 70 %" for nitramide, which the thesis states for a different run.
- **Model B's X.** It peaks at 125.2 mM-N (25.0 % of N) at 77.8 min. It falls 39 mM-N short of the remainder at 30 min and exceeds it by 15, 63, 35, 29 and 13 mM-N at 60-180 min. Two things limit what that excess means:
  - Part of it is model B's misfit, not hidden-pool size. X minus the deficit equals the observed data minus model B's observed species. At 90 min X (122.5) exceeds even the whole deficit (95.7) by 26.8 mM-N, because model B under-predicts NO3 + NO2 there (149.3 against 184.1 mM-N). The 62.7 mM-N excess at 90 min is 26.8 of misfit plus the 35.9 of measured intermediates.
  - Noise. If the +-10 % bars are 1 sigma, the deficit carries 20-32 mM-N (27.6, 20.0, 21.8, 26.3, 30.2, 31.9 at 30-180 min). The 60 and 180 min excesses (15.0 and 13.1) are within that. Only 90 min clears 1 sigma clearly, 120 min marginally, and 150 min sits at about 1 sigma.
- **What holds up.** X's timing: it peaks at 78 min, after the remainder, which is highest at 30-60 min. And X exceeds the remainder at 90-150 min, although the size of that excess is uncertain for the reasons above. For the plausibility rule (`max_hidden_fraction_of_total_N` = 0.30), the data-implied unobserved pool falls from 28.4 % of N (the deficit at 60 min) to 20.6 % once the measured intermediates are removed; model B's X peaks at 25.0 %. A large unmeasured pool remains either way, so an "implausibly large" argument has to rest on X's size and timing against this remainder, not on X's existence.
- **N basis.** Hyponitrite is counted as 2 N per molecule. The thesis does not state the basis, but it is the sound default: the 2-N gases in the same figure are plotted per molecule (doubled, they reproduce the N-equivalent concentrations to 0.2 mM-N), the ion-chromatography calibration is a mass concentration of the anion, and the thesis writes the hyponitrite reactions per molecule. The mass balance does not decide it: at 1 N the coverage would be 0.08-0.26, also feasible.
- **Lower bounds.** NH2OH can react with nitrite before analysis, and hyponitrite decomposes to N2O (thesis printed pp. 140 and 145), so the measured intermediates are lower bounds.
- **NH3.** The NH3 rates do not invert to the thesis Diag. 12.3 markers (see the cross-check above), so the liquid-phase data behind Diag. 12.1 differ from the plotted figure for NH3 at least. Using the thesis-copy deficit instead of Daniel's moves the coverage at 120 min from 0.380 to 0.406.

### Diag. 12.3 error bars are a uniform +-10 %

Every measurable error bar in the thesis copy of Katsounaros 2012 Fig. 1 is +-10.0 % of the plotted value, for all five species and all times. The analyst's 18 resolved bars give a relative half-width of 0.0997 +- 0.0010; the verifier re-measured 13 with its own calibration and got 0.0997 +- 0.0007 (range 0.098-0.101). The bars span 15-309 mM, so per-point replicate statistics would scatter far more: the plotting program applied a fixed percentage. That matches the thesis's statement that its experimental error "was of the order of 10%" (printed p. 81). Neither the caption nor the text says what the bars are (1 sigma, 2 sigma or a range). For a noise model, use a relative sigma of 0.10 (0.05 if the bars are 2 sigma or a maximum-error range) plus an absolute floor for points near zero, where a relative sigma vanishes. Folder: `katsounaros2009thesis_diag12p3_errorbars`.

### SPEC 7b quotes model B's X peak late and low

`docs/SPEC.md` section 7b says model B's hidden X "peaks at 122 mM-N (24%) around 85 min". The repo simulator with the W3 constants gives 125.2 mM-N (25.0 % of N) at 77.8 min; 122.5 is the value at the 90 min sample. The W3 row in SPEC section 5 and the W3 world notes say "about 24%". The SPEC conclusion that model B places the pool later than the data deficit still holds.

### Dortsiou Al "nitramide" is a mass-balance deficit

The authors plot a nitramide (NH2NO2) series in Diag. 9.3. The thesis says nitramide could not be quantified (no standard; printed p. 117) and that it accounts for the mass-balance loss (p. 163). The plotted values equal 50 mM minus the five measured species at every time: the six series sum to 49.97-50.08 mM. The series is therefore stored as `quality.reported_n_loss`, and the measured species close at 0.88-0.94. The authors' chapter 9 rate constants were fitted with NH2NO2 as an observed species, so any reuse of their k2, k4 and k5 inherits a "measurement" that is really a deficit. For the hidden-intermediate question this is a loss term, not an observation.

Both Dortsiou captions are exactly 0.40 V less negative than thesis Table 6.1: Zn -2.24 vs -2.64 V, Al -2.13 vs -2.53 V. The other Zn captions, Diags. 9.2 and 9.4, repeat 2.24 V (9.4 without a sign). The Zn nitrate decay (0.0285 min-1) is slower than the Table 6.1 k (0.0374). The Al decay (0.0292) is faster than Table 6.1 (0.0203), although -2.13 V would be the less negative potential. Caption values are kept and flagged `potential_unverified`. The chapter 9 Zn constants were fitted to Diag. 9.4, possibly a different run, and miss the Diag. 6.6 points (RMSE 2.7 mM).

### Katsounaros Diag. 6.4: text and figure disagree on nitrite

The text quotes a nitrite maximum of 1.15 mM at 15 min. The plotted circle sits at 0.60 mM, with its full outline visible (0.55-0.65 mM). The data follow the figure. Closure runs 1.05-1.10 at 30-90 min. That is within the thesis's stated error of about 10 %, and the text's own selectivities sum to 105 %. Anyone fitting the N balance should allow for it. Diag. 6.7's 1 h endpoint at -2.4 V does not match the 60 min point here. ## 5. Before fitting

These gaps sit between the new files and the investigator. They are documented here, not fixed in code.

- **Unmapped channels.** The evaluator matches each measured channel to a mechanism species by name (`default_observation_map` in `src/nernstwork/canonical.py`) and rejects a candidate that leaves a channel without a species. `NH3_trap` (Gao), `N2` and `N2O` (theses, Daniel's files) therefore need species in the mechanism or an explicit `observation_map`. Every series is in mM-N, so N2 and N2O must be N-equivalent species with explicit stoichiometric coefficients: 2 NO2 -> N2 is written as 2 mM-N of nitrite giving 2 mM-N of N2, and the rate constant has to be stated in that convention (see the W5 case in section 2).
- **No RHE potentials.** Every new file has `potential_V_vs_RHE: null`; Ag/AgCl and SCE values are stored as reported. Work across potentials needs an applied-potential covariate within one source (same reference electrode, cell and iR situation), not a converted RHE value. Gao's potentials are vs SCE without iR compensation at roughly 50-90 mA cm-2 (stated for Fig. 2e, derived for S14-S15), so even within Gao the applied potential is not the interfacial potential.
- **Single-dataset runs only today.** `src/nernstwork/investigation.py` accepts exactly one development dataset per run; held-out runs are read only by `final_evaluate`. The potential, pH and concentration series (Gao S13-S15, the thesis series still to extract) need a joint fit with shared parameters before they can test potential or concentration dependence.
- **pH is the initial value only.** `conditions.pH` holds the starting pH. In Gao's Fig. 2e run the cathode loop goes from pH 7 to 12.65 (stated only for that run). In Dortsiou ch. 5 the carbonate electrolyte goes from 9.65 to about 11.3 (Bi runs). A pH-dependent mechanism needs pH(t) or a buffer model.
- **Inferred conditions are stored as values.** The schema has no "inferred" marker, so an inferred number in `conditions` looks like a stated one. Inferred: Dortsiou pH 9.65 and volume 10 mL; Gao feed volume 50 mL for Figs. S13-S15 and 2a; Barrera 7.0 mL (inputs only). The metas' notes and flags say which values are inferred; check them before treating a value as measured.
- **Noise model.** Digitization error (0.03-0.6 mM, section 1) is far below run-to-run variation. The thesis authors give about ±10 % experimental error (Katsounaros Diag. 6.4 text; Dortsiou accepts about 10 % simulation error in ch. 9), the Katsounaros 2012 run carries uniform ±10 % error bars of unstated type (section 2), and Barrera's triplicates differ by 8-32 % relative in NH3 FE. A fit that uses digitization error as sigma will overstate what these data can resolve; a relative sigma of 0.10 (0.05 if the bars are 2 sigma) with an absolute floor near zero is the better default until replicates say otherwise.
